This project asks how hourly bike rental volume varies with weather conditions, working-day status, and hour of day in the 2011-2012 Capital Bikeshare data. It uses the public UCI Bike Sharing dataset under CC BY 4.0. The source, DOI, and SHA-256 of the unchanged hourly file are recorded in the Data Card.

The file contains 17,379 rows and 17 columns. The analysis used basic schema, range, missing-value, duplicate, anomaly, and leakage checks. There were no missing cells, duplicate rows, duplicate date-hour keys, or invalid values in the checked ranges. Large rental counts were inspected rather than automatically removed.

The equality cnt = casual + registered holds on every row. If cnt is the prediction target, casual and registered should not be predictors because together they reveal the total. No prediction model was trained.

Mean rentals were 204.87 per hour in clear/few-cloud weather and 111.58 in light rain/snow. Working days peaked at 17:00, while non-working days peaked at 13:00.

These are descriptive patterns from one system and a historical period. Weather comparisons do not establish causal effects. Also, completed rentals do not measure everyone who wanted a bike. The notebook, PDF, data documentation, result tables, and AI-use log are included in the repository.
